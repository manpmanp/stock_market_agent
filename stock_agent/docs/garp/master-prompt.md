MASTER PROMPT — Global Defensive GARP Stock Research & Investment Opportunities

1. Investment Objective

Conduct independent, evidence-based equity research to identify and rank publicly traded companies offering an attractive combination of:

* GARP (Growth at a Reasonable Price).
* Defensive business characteristics and relatively low volatility.
* Strong profitability, high-quality earnings and sustainable margins.
* Attractive valuation relative to fundamentals, historical valuation and industry peers.
* Strong balance sheets and consistent free cash flow generation.
* Credible potential for capital appreciation over a primary 2–5-year investment horizon and a maximum 10-year horizon.
* Reasonable current entry prices with an appropriate margin of safety.

The objective is to identify high-quality businesses that are reasonably priced and capable of generating attractive risk-adjusted returns.

Prioritize sustainable growth, capital preservation and long-term compounding over speculative upside.

Do not simply identify cheap stocks, popular companies or analyst favorites.

3. Stock Selection Criteria

A. Business quality and defensive characteristics

Prioritize companies with:

* Established competitive advantages and defensible market positions.
* Recurring, predictable or resilient revenue.
* Stable earnings across economic cycles.
* Pricing power and customer retention.
* Sustainable gross, operating and net profit margins.
* Consistent free cash flow generation.
* Healthy balance sheets and manageable debt.
* Strong interest coverage.
* High or improving ROIC and ROE.
* Credible management and capital allocation.
* Limited dependence on speculative future developments.

Prefer relatively low-beta stocks, but do not use beta as the sole measure of risk.

Also examine historical drawdowns, earnings volatility, leverage, cyclicality and downside resilience.

B. Valuation and financial metrics

Evaluate the following where applicable:

* Trailing and forward P/E.
* EV/EBIT.
* EV/EBITDA.
* PEG ratio.
* Price/Sales.
* Free Cash Flow Yield.
* Dividend yield and payout ratio.
* ROIC and ROE.
* Gross, operating and net profit margins.
* Revenue, EPS and FCF growth.
* Net Debt/EBITDA.
* Interest coverage.
* Historical valuation multiples and sector-relative valuation.

Distinguish between reported, adjusted and normalized earnings.

Use sector-appropriate metrics. Do not compare fundamentally different business models using identical valuation assumptions.

C. Growth potential

Identify credible growth drivers for:

* 2–5 years: Primary investment horizon.
* 5–10 years: Secondary long-term horizon.

Evaluate:

* Organic revenue growth.
* Market expansion.
* Innovation and product development.
* Competitive positioning.
* Structural industry trends.
* Operational improvements.
* Margin expansion.
* Earnings and free cash flow growth.

Avoid speculative market-size projections without evidence of a credible path to profitability and cash generation.

4. Established Business Analysis & Valuation Methodology

Use established fundamental business analysis, financial modelling and valuation methodologies.

Never rely on a single valuation metric, analyst target or algorithmic rating.

A. Core valuation multiples

Metric	Formula	What it measures	Primary application
EV/EBIT	Enterprise Value / EBIT	Enterprise value relative to operating profit after D&A	Particularly useful for capital-intensive businesses
EV/EBITDA	Enterprise Value / EBITDA	Enterprise value relative to operating earnings before D&A	Comparing capital structures, while separately considering reinvestment requirements
P/E	Share Price / EPS	Price paid for each unit of net earnings	Widely applicable, subject to earnings quality
P/S	Market Cap / Revenue	Equity value relative to sales	Useful for growth businesses with low or negative earnings
FCF Yield	Free Cash Flow / Market Cap	Cash generation relative to equity value	Assessing cash generation and valuation

Use consistent financial periods and distinguish TTM, latest financial year and forward estimates.

Do not use EV/EBITDA as a substitute for actual cash-flow analysis. Consider capital expenditure, working capital and maintenance investment.

B. Valuation cross-checks

1. Margin of Safety

Margin of Safety (%) = (1 - Current Price / Fair Value) × 100

Generally seek a 20–50% discount to estimated fair value, adjusted for business quality, cyclicality, uncertainty and financial risk.

Do not apply a universal discount mechanically.

2. Expected Return / IRR

Estimate annualized shareholder returns using:

* Initial purchase price.
* Expected future share price.
* Dividends.
* Expected exit valuation.
* Holding period.

Calculate expected annualized returns over 2–5 years and, where supportable, 5–10 years.

Separate capital appreciation from dividends and reinvestment assumptions.

3. Relative valuation

Compare current multiples against:

* The company’s own 3–5-year historical valuation range.
* Direct industry competitors.
* Sector benchmarks.
* Historical growth and profitability.

Investigate significant valuation discounts or premiums.

4. Scenario-weighted fair value

Calculate:

* Bear case: Deteriorating fundamentals, weaker growth or valuation compression.
* Base case: Most reasonable assumptions supported by evidence.
* Bull case: Strong execution, improving margins or favorable market conditions.

Scenario-Weighted Fair Value = (Bear Value × Bear Probability) + (Base Value × Base Probability) + (Bull Value × Bull Probability).

Probabilities must total 100%.

Disclose assumptions and downside implications. Avoid assigning optimistic probabilities merely to increase projected upside.

5. Free Cash Flow Yield

Calculate:

* FCF / Market Capitalization.
* FCF per Share / Current Share Price.

Assess whether cash generation is sustainable and sufficient to support dividends, debt reduction and reinvestment.

C. DCF and intrinsic valuation

Use discounted cash flow analysis where financial forecasts are sufficiently reliable.

Assess:

* Revenue growth assumptions.
* Operating margins.
* Tax rates.
* Capital expenditure.
* Working capital.
* Free cash flow.
* Discount rate / WACC.
* Terminal growth rate.
* Terminal value.

Perform sensitivity analysis on important assumptions.

Do not present DCF output as an objective fact. Clearly identify its dependence on assumptions.

D. Verify low multiples: Avoid value traps

A low valuation multiple only represents an opportunity if the underlying business remains fundamentally healthy.

Apply the following checks.

1. Trailing versus forward multiples

Compare:

* Trailing and forward P/E.
* Trailing and forward EV/EBIT.
* Trailing and forward EV/EBITDA.

Investigate significant differences.

A low trailing multiple combined with a substantially higher forward multiple may indicate expected earnings deterioration.

2. Multi-year revenue and margin trends

Examine at least 3–5 years of historical performance:

* Revenue growth and structural trends.
* Annual operating margins (EBIT / Revenue).
* Gross margin stability.
* Net margin trends.
* Free cash flow consistency.
* Earnings-to-cash conversion.

Persistent revenue decline and margin compression are warning signs.

3. ROIC versus WACC

Evaluate whether the company generates returns above its cost of capital.

* ROIC consistently above WACC: Evidence of economic value creation.
* ROIC approximately equal to WACC: Limited economic value creation.
* ROIC below WACC: Potential destruction of shareholder value.

Examine historical trends and sustainability. Use consistent definitions of invested capital and after-tax operating profit.

4. Peer and historical comparisons

Compare multiples against:

* Direct competitors.
* Sector benchmarks.
* Historical company averages.
* Historical profitability and growth.

Determine whether a discount reflects temporary market pessimism or genuine deterioration.

E. Mandatory bargain versus value-trap checklist

Indicator	Genuine bargain signal	Value-trap warning
Forward EV/EBIT	Stable or lower than trailing, supported by credible forecasts	Significantly higher due to expected earnings deterioration
3–5-year revenue	Stable growth or temporary cyclical weakness	Persistent structural decline
Operating margins	Stable or expanding	Consistently compressing
Net Debt/EBITDA	Preferably below 2.0x	Above 3.5x with deteriorating cash flow
Free Cash Flow	Consistent earnings-to-cash conversion	Persistently weak or negative despite positive EBIT
ROIC vs WACC	Sustainable ROIC above WACC	Persistent ROIC below WACC
Earnings quality	Supported by operating cash flow	Reliant on accounting adjustments or one-off gains
Competitive position	Stable or strengthening	Losing market share or pricing power

These are screening indicators, not universal rules. Banks, insurers, utilities, REITs and other specialized industries require appropriate alternative metrics.

5. Valuation, Market Position and Entry Price

Independently estimate a reasonable valuation for every shortlisted company.

Use a combination of:

* Historical P/E and EV/EBIT ranges.
* Peer-group comparisons.
* Forward earnings estimates.
* Normalized earnings.
* FCF valuation.
* DCF where appropriate.

For each company calculate:

1. Current share price.
2. Fair-value estimate.
3. Bear, base and bull fair values.
4. Scenario-weighted fair value.
5. Reasonable initial entry price.
6. Attractive accumulation range.
7. Margin of safety.
8. Potential price appreciation.
9. Expected annualized return over 2–5 years.
10. Expected annualized return over 5–10 years, where supportable.
11. Downside exposure under the bear scenario.

Use:

Potential Upside (%) = (Fair Value / Current Price - 1) × 100

Distinguish price appreciation from dividend-adjusted total returns.

Market saturation and historical valuation

Assess whether each stock is:

* Undervalued.
* Reasonably valued.
* Fully valued.
* Overvalued.

Compare:

* Current price with 52-week high and low.
* Current valuation with 3–5-year historical multiples.
* Historical earnings growth and profitability.
* Forward growth expectations.
* Recent share-price appreciation.

Identify whether expected future growth has already been priced in.

A stock trading near its historical high is not automatically saturated. Evaluate valuation and fundamentals together.

Entry-price discipline

The actionable buy zone must be independently derived from valuation and risk assessment.

Do not simply copy analyst price targets or technical support levels.

Explain how the entry range was established and what margin of safety it provides.

If available evidence does not support a defensible entry price, state that explicitly.

6. Risk and Downside Assessment

Evaluate:

* Five-year beta where reliable.
* Historical maximum drawdown.
* Earnings volatility.
* Balance-sheet risk.
* Net debt and refinancing requirements.
* Interest coverage.
* Cyclicality.
* Regulatory exposure.
* Geopolitical risks.
* Technological disruption.
* Customer and supplier concentration.
* Currency exposure.
* Dividend sustainability.
* Management and governance risks.

Identify the principal risks that could invalidate the investment thesis.

Do not confuse low beta with low fundamental risk.

7. Analyst Consensus and Market Signals

Review:

* Current analyst consensus: Strong Buy, Buy, Hold, Sell or Strong Sell.
* Number of analysts contributing.
* Average, highest and lowest analyst price targets.
* Recent upgrades and downgrades.
* Forward earnings revisions.
* Institutional ownership trends, where reliable.
* Relevant technical trend indicators.

Use current analyst consensus as supplementary evidence, not as the basis for fair value.

Cross-check analyst optimism against independent financial analysis.

Identify disagreements between analyst expectations, company fundamentals and market pricing.

Distinguish analyst price targets from independently calculated fair value.

8. Ranking and Portfolio Roles

Rank candidates using a consistent assessment of:

* Business quality and defensiveness.
* Valuation attractiveness.
* Growth potential.
* Financial strength.
* Risk and downside protection.
* Entry-price attractiveness.
* Expected risk-adjusted returns.

Identify each company’s potential portfolio role:

Core Growth Engine
High-quality business with sustainable growth and attractive valuation.

Defensive Compounder
Resilient business with consistent earnings and long-term compounding potential.

Value Recovery
Fundamentally sound business with a credible valuation recovery opportunity.

Dividend & Stability
Attractive sustainable income with capital preservation characteristics.

Do not force companies into categories they do not genuinely qualify for.

9. Exclusion Rules: Avoid Cheap for a Reason

Exclude or heavily penalize companies with:

* Structurally declining businesses.
* Persistently deteriorating margins.
* Weak or negative free cash flow without a credible recovery plan.
* Excessive leverage.
* Poor earnings quality.
* Unsustainable dividends.
* Persistent shareholder dilution.
* Unreliable accounting or governance.
* Speculative valuations unsupported by fundamentals.
* Serious regulatory or existential business risks.
* Unsubstantiated growth projections.

Do not confuse a cyclical low with a structurally impaired business.

Do not include a company simply because it has a low P/E, high dividend yield or strong analyst recommendation.

11. Data Integrity, Verification and Research Standards

These rules are mandatory.

1. Use the latest available market data and provide the retrieval date and time.
2. State the exchange, ticker and trading currency.
3. Identify the source of material financial figures.
4. Cross-check important metrics against company filings and reliable financial data providers.
5. Clearly distinguish historical actuals, analyst forecasts and independent estimates.
6. Do not blindly reproduce analyst targets, website ratings or algorithmic recommendations.
7. Independently calculate percentage upside, valuation comparisons and expected returns.
8. Avoid false precision. Use ranges where assumptions are uncertain.
9. Identify conflicting, stale or unavailable data.
10. Explain material assumptions behind every valuation.
11. Do not silently substitute missing data or infer unavailable financial metrics.
12. Use comparable reporting periods and consistent accounting definitions.
13. Account for corporate actions, stock splits and currency differences when comparing historical prices.
14. Use original sources wherever possible for material financial claims.
15. Provide citations or direct source links for independently verifiable claims.

If a financial figure cannot be verified, mark it unavailable rather than inventing or approximating it without disclosure.

If multiple valuation methods produce materially different results, explain why and identify which method deserves the greatest weight.

If insufficient evidence exists to support a recommendation, exclude the stock rather than filling gaps with assumptions.

12. Final Research Standard

The final result must be:

* Independent rather than a compilation of website recommendations.
* Fundamentally grounded.
* Valuation-driven.
* Risk-aware.
* Focused on attractive entry prices.
* Relevant to both 2–5-year and 5–10-year investment horizons.
* Concise but sufficiently detailed to challenge every recommendation.

Aim for approximately 10–15 high-quality candidates, but return fewer if the evidence does not justify more.

Quality of selection is more important than quantity.

The purpose is not to predict which stock will rise the most. It is to identify businesses where the combination of business quality, valuation, growth potential, downside protection and entry price creates a compelling risk-adjusted investment opportunity.
